Your other accounts.

Your pension isn’t the only money you retire with. Here’s what else you have, in plain English.

What you have

  • Deferred comp: the 457 Money you choose to put in from each paycheck. You put money inPre-tax or RothNo penalty once you leave the job
  • Deferred comp: the 401(k) A second account, with its own separate limit. You put money inPre-tax or RothPenalty-free at 50, or after 25 years
  • NYCE IRA The City’s own low-cost IRA, for you and your spouse. You put money inTraditional or RothTakes rollovers
  • Union annuity An account the City pays into for you through the UFA or UFOA. The City puts money inPaid out when you retire
  • VSF and the banked variable $12,000 a year after 20 years, plus a lump sum for each year past 20. Part of your pension; the calculators already include it. The City pays it

The 457 and the 401(k) are both part of the NYC Deferred Compensation Plan, run by the City’s Office of Labor Relations. Voya keeps the records and runs the website and app, which is why a lot of guys call the whole thing “Voya.”

Common questions

What’s the difference between the 457 and the 401(k)?

They work almost the same way. You pick a percentage of each paycheck, it comes out automatically, and you choose how it’s invested. The big differences:

457Once you leave the job, you can take money out at any age with no 10% penalty. You still pay income tax on pre-tax money.
401(k)Take money out before 59½ and there can be a 10% penalty. But retired public safety members can withdraw penalty-free at age 50 or after 25 years, whichever comes first.

The 457 also has a special catch-up in the last few years before retirement (see below). The 401(k) doesn’t.

Can I put money in both?

Yes. Each has its own limit, so you can put the full amount in the 457 and the full amount in the 401(k) in the same year. Most members don’t know this.

How much can I put in?

2026 limits, set by the IRS:

Your age in 2026Each plan
Under 50$24,500
50 to 59, or 64 and up$32,500
60 to 63$35,750

Those are per plan. Double them if you use both the 457 and the 401(k).

The IRS raises these most years. The 2027 limits come out in November.

Through payroll, you can put in anywhere from 1% to 75% of your pay.

Pre-tax or Roth: what’s the difference?
Pre-taxLowers your taxes now. You pay income tax when you take it out in retirement, on what you put in and what it earned.
RothNo tax break now. In retirement, what you put in and what it earned come out tax-free, if the account is at least 5 years old and you’re 59½ or older.

Which one makes more sense depends on whether you expect your tax rate to be higher now or in retirement. Keep in mind your pension is taxable federal income, so it fills up the lower tax brackets once you retire. You can split your money between both, and you can change it anytime.

This site doesn’t tell anyone which to pick. A tax advisor can look at your whole picture.

I made over $150,000 last year. Does anything change?

Only for the extra “catch-up” money at 50 and up. Starting in 2026, if your City wages the year before were over $150,000, any catch-up contributions have to go in as Roth, not pre-tax. Your regular contributions up to the base limit can still be pre-tax.

With overtime, a lot of members and most officers will cross this line. The threshold goes up with inflation.

What is the special 457 catch-up?

In the 457 only, the three years before your “normal retirement age” under the plan, you may be able to put in up to double the regular limit, to make up for years you didn’t put in the max. The Plan calls it Deferral Acceleration for Retirement (DAR).

You can’t use it in the same year as the age-50 catch-up, and you only get one 3-year window. Call the Plan before you use it, so you pick the right years.

When can I take money out?

While you’re still working: generally not before 59½. There are narrow exceptions: a 457 unforeseeable emergency, a 401(k) hardship withdrawal, up to $5,000 after a birth or adoption, and a one-time withdrawal of a small 457 balance. Or take a loan (below).

After you leave the job: whenever you want, starting about 45 days after you separate. Take it all, take part, set up monthly payments, or leave it invested.

  • 457: no 10% penalty at any age.
  • 401(k): no 10% penalty once you’re 50 or have 25 years, as a retired public safety member. Before that, there can be one.
  • Roth money: tax-free once the account is 5 years old and you’re 59½.

You don’t have to touch pre-tax money until 73. That’s when the IRS makes you start taking a minimum amount each year. Roth money in the plan has no required minimum.

Can I borrow from it?

Yes, from the pre-tax money in either plan, not the Roth. You pay it back through payroll, with interest that goes back into your own account.

Money you borrow isn’t invested while it’s out, and leaving the job with a loan unpaid can make it taxable.

Can I use it to buy back time for my pension?

Yes. Pre-tax 457 or 401(k) money can pay for a pension buyback, such as military time, moved straight to the Fire Pension Fund with no tax. Roth money can’t be used.

What is the union annuity?

An account the City pays into for you, through your union. You don’t put anything in. It’s separate from the pension and from deferred comp.

  • The City deposits money on a regular schedule (every 28 days, for the UFA’s fund).
  • You choose how it’s invested, and you get statements.
  • When you’re promoted to officer, it moves from the UFA’s fund to the UFOA’s.
  • You can take it when you retire, as cash or rolled into the Deferred Comp 401(k) (it has a special account for union annuities) or an IRA.
  • Name a beneficiary. If you die, the balance goes to them.

What about the NYCE IRA?

The City’s own IRA, for current and former City employees and their spouses. Traditional or Roth. It uses the same low-cost funds as deferred comp, with no cost to open it.

Since July 2025 you can put money in through payroll. The limit is much lower than the 457 or 401(k): $7,500 in 2026, or $8,600 at 50 and up.

Its biggest use: when you retire, you can roll your deferred comp or union annuity into it and keep the same cheap funds.

What does it cost?

$20 a quarter (waived your first year), charged once even if you have both plans, plus 0.04% a year on your balance. The funds’ own fees are low too: the S&P 500 index fund costs about 0.04% a year.

What can I invest in?
  • Pre-arranged portfolios: pick the one closest to the year you plan to retire, and it gets more conservative as that year gets closer.
  • Index funds: the S&P 500, mid-size and small companies, international stocks, bonds, and a socially responsible fund.
  • Stable Income Fund: steady interest, low risk. Not FDIC insured.
  • Self-directed brokerage: up to 20% of your balance in outside funds, once you have $5,000.

Moving money out of a fund within 32 days of putting it in costs a 2% fee.

Who gets my money if I die?

Whoever you named as beneficiary, and you name them separately for each plan. Check yours after a marriage, divorce or new baby. Your union annuity has its own beneficiary too.

How do I sign up or check my balance?

Online at nyc.gov/deferredcomp, or in the Voya app. Or call the Deferred Compensation Plan at 212-306-7760 (888-DCP-3113 outside NYC).

Contributions start about two weeks after you sign up.

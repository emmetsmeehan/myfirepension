After you retire

Your last tour isn't the end of the paperwork. Here's what to expect from the Fire Pension Fund in the first few months, roughly in the order it happens.

Based on the Fund's own retirement checklist. It's a good map, but confirm anything with the Fund before you make a decision.

What happens next

Your first pension check

Pension checks are paid on the last day of the month, unless that day falls on a weekend or holiday.

For the first stretch you won't get your full pension. The Fund pays you 90% of your estimated maximum pension until your pension is finalized. Budget for the first few months with that in mind.

Your pension number

Your pension number is assigned after your first payment is processed. It comes by mail, about 14 to 30 days after your first check. Hang on to that letter.

Retiree Self Service (RSS)

Once you have your pension number, about 30 days after your first payment, you can sign up for the Fund's Retiree Self Service.

The excess: you have 60 days to decide

About 30 days after your first pension check, you'll get a Notice of Excess. You should respond within 60 days and tell the Fund what you want to do with that money. Your choices are:

  • Take it as a lump sum
  • Roll it over
  • Add it to your pension
  • Use it to offset a loan

This is a big decision with a short clock, so don't let the deadline sneak up on you.

Not sure what your excess is? Your taxable and non-taxable excess are on the Fund's member portal and on your annual pension statement.

Excess and Option 2

If you add your excess to your pension and pick Option 2, my understanding is your spouse keeps getting the full amount after you pass, excess included, since Option 2 covers your whole retirement allowance. The trade-off is that Option 2 lowers your whole pension while you're alive. When the Fund sends your option letter, ask them to show Option 2 both ways, with the excess added to your pension and with it taken as a lump sum, so you can compare real numbers.

Retirement loan Optional

If you want a retirement loan, you have to request it within 60 days of retirement. Contact the Fund's Member Transactions Unit: [Member Transactions email and phone]

Terminal leave

The Fund will send you a Terminal Leave Disbursement Form after you retire. The terminal leave payment itself comes from FDNY, not the Fund, and it takes a while: at least 6 to 8 months after your retirement date. Don't count on it for early bills.

Moving or changing banks

If you change your bank account or move, contact the Fund as soon as you can: [Fund phone number]. If you're moving out of state, look at how your new state taxes pensions first: [link to a pension tax by state guide]

Before you hand in your badge

A few items have to happen while you're still active, and they shape what happens after.

ESS goes away

You lose ESS access when you return your badge or on your effective retirement date. If you want your pay history for the calculators here, grab your ESS screenshots before then.

Terminal leave and DCP

You can defer up to 75% of your terminal leave lump sum into your NYCDCP plans (401(k), 457, Roth IRA). To do it, you have to change your DCP deferral percentage yourself in your last 14 days on the job.

Health benefits

Your health benefits application goes to the FDNY Health Benefits Unit, not the Pension Fund. The Fund doesn't handle health benefits, so send those questions to the Health Benefits Unit.
